# Supplier Stability Framework, Version 1 Scope

## What this framework is for

Supplier performance rarely breaks because one number was wrong. The harder problem is when a supplier looks healthy on a summary scorecard, while commitment dates are drifting, late orders are still open, and the planning team is losing room to recover.

The Supplier Stability Framework is built to make that friction easier to see. It works from purchase-order history and turns a few practical delivery signals into a clearer supplier conversation.

The current Friction Index brings together four signals:

1. Late-arrival frequency
2. Open-late exposure
3. Commitment integrity
4. Delay severity

In practice, it gives procurement and Supplier Managers a starting point for asking better questions. Where is delivery friction building up? Which supplier relationships need a closer look? What should be reviewed before the next supplier meeting or category discussion?

## How to use it

The framework is designed to sit next to normal OTD reporting, not replace it. A Friction Index result is most useful when it is reviewed with the underlying order lines, current business impact, and the people who know the supplier relationship.

A high score can help bring the right supplier into a governance or category discussion. A critical project issue may still need immediate attention based on project impact, even if the current score looks calm.

The public dashboard uses synthetic demonstration data. It is there to show the workflow, fields, and logic without exposing company or supplier information.

## Criticality and quantity

Component criticality is still experimental in the current release. The next version is being shaped around how manufacturing teams can tailor criticality settings to their own stability needs, production output, and operating conditions.

The dashboard currently displays component criticality, project, category, and quantity data so teams can filter and review the right order lines. The next version will explore how criticality and partial-shipment quantity can be incorporated more directly into prioritization.

For example, a late custom component and a late readily available item may create very different operational pressure. The framework is moving toward representing that difference more clearly, while keeping the logic transparent and practical.

## Quality context

Material Discrepancy data can be reviewed alongside delivery friction. It helps teams see whether a supplier relationship also carries quality-event or recovery-time pressure.

The upcoming development work will focus on making delivery and quality context easier to review together, while keeping the drivers behind each result visible to the user.

## Data and review

The framework works best when purchase-order dates, receipt dates, and open-order status are maintained with care. Good data makes the discussion more useful. Human review keeps the result connected to what is actually happening in the business.

Upcoming work will add clearer data-confidence guidance, more support for partial fulfillment, and stronger review workflows for different manufacturing environments.
